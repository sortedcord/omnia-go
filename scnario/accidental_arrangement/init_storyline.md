You arrive at Nelly’s apartment five minutes after Mia’s last message telling you to “please just come up before she changes her mind.”

The door opens before you can knock properly.

Mia stands there immediately, like she’s been waiting behind it the entire time.

“Oh thank god,” she says under her breath, already stepping aside to pull you inside before you can say anything.

The apartment is warm, slightly cluttered, and somehow gives the impression that someone has been trying very hard to make it look less cluttered for the past several hours.

There are papers stacked neatly on the coffee table. Two mugs in the sink. A hoodie draped over the back of a chair like it was placed there intentionally.

Mia notices you looking.

“See? We already have visual storytelling,” she says casually, like this is somehow reassuring.

You stare at her.

She presses her lips together for a second, then exhales dramatically.

“Okay. Before you say no,” she starts quickly, pointing at you like she already knows you’re about to interrupt, “this is temporary. Like… very temporary.”

From somewhere deeper in the apartment, a muffled voice appears:

“Mia, please stop describing it like a hostage situation.”

You hear movement. Then silence. Then movement again, like someone changed direction halfway through leaving a room.

Mia lowers her voice slightly.

“She’s spiraling.”

Right on cue, Nelly appears from the hallway.

She almost looks composed at first glance. Oversized hoodie, loose pants, hair partially pinned back like she attempted to organize it halfway through getting ready. But the second she notices you standing there, something visibly short-circuits behind her eyes.

“Oh,” she says.

A beat.

“Hi.”

Her voice comes out more prepared than natural, like she rehearsed the word beforehand and still wasn’t satisfied with the result.

One hand tightens slightly around the sleeve covering part of her fingers.

“You actually came,” she adds, then immediately winces a little. “Not that I thought you wouldn’t, that just sounded more accusatory than I meant it to.”

Mia quietly walks past both of you toward the kitchen like her work here is already done.

Nelly notices too late.

“Mia.”

“Mm?”

“You said you’d stay for this part.”

“I am staying,” Mia replies easily, opening the fridge. “Emotionally.”

Nelly stares at her for a second like she’s genuinely considering murder as a concept.

Then she looks back at you.

There’s a pause long enough to become noticeable.

“I made notes,” she says suddenly.

Another pause.

“I hear how bad that sounds now.”
